Paul Thomas's continuous-time sigma-delta on Tiny Tapeout

A first-order, single-ended, continuous-time (active-RC) modulator on the 3.3 V analog rail, clocked at 50 MHz, with no on-chip decimator. This page covers how it is built, how it is fitted to the Tiny Tapeout analog frame and pin path, and what carries over to our switched-capacitor incremental design.

Simulation and extracted-layout results only. No silicon yet.

Signal path

The input pin feeds a 132 kΩ poly resistor into the OTA's virtual ground. Because the input is a current summed at that node, the input range is set purely by resistor ratios: RIN maps ±1.65 V about 1.65 V onto ±12.5 µA. ROFF (158.4 kΩ to ground) cancels the standing current from the 1.65 V mid-scale sitting above the 0.9 V common mode. The feedback DAC is a 20 kΩ resistor driven by five NMOS switches in return-to-zero fashion: data half-cycle to VREFP or VREFN, return half-cycle to VCM.

Floorplan and pin map

He assembled the top cell directly in frame coordinates, so the cell's die matches the 2×2 3.3 V template and its pin legs land on the template's exact pin positions. Power arrives on three full-height met4 stripes. The blocks alone would fit a 1×2, but routing corridors and clearance rules spread the layout to about 315 µm wide, so he paid for 2×2.

How the design is fitted to Tiny Tapeout

Each row pairs a platform constraint with the choice it drove.

TT constraintWhat he did
Analog pin path: under 500 Ω and 5 pF through tt_asw_3v3The input is a 132k resistor into a virtual ground. With no switched-capacitor sampling there is no kickback through the mux, and the 500 Ω switch is about a 0.4% gain error. The resistor input also filters aliases on its own.
Analog pins cost €40 each for two, then €100 eachTwo pins. VOFF and vcm/vrefp monitor pins were added (5 pins, €380) and removed the same day (back to €80). The references are inferred from the transfer function instead: ones-density 0.5 gives vcm, the full-scale points give the vrefp−vcm span, and a VAPWR sweep checks ratiometry.
Six analog pins at most; digital pins are freeClock on the normal clk pin; bitstream on uo[0]/uo[1] as complementary Q/Q̄ for a pseudo-differential FPGA receiver. Nothing at 50 MHz goes through the analog mux.
Unequal rise and fall delays on TT IOThe feedback DAC never leaves the chip. RZ makes edge errors identical every cycle, so they become a fixed gain error. Clock duty error is also a gain shift, and the level shifter is gated at ≤ 3%. The bitstream is captured one bit per clock, so pin edge shape does not enter the signal.
Clock speed through the TT clock path50 MHz, based on the urish charge-pump projects measuring 62–100 MHz through that path in silicon. The stated fallback is a lower fs, not an output demultiplexer.

Lessons he paid for

Each item came from a failure in his own flow and is recorded in DESIGN.md with a date.

1

Comparator sampling instant is part of loop timing

A StrongARM samples when its tail turns on, which was the falling edge of clk33. That put the decision half a cycle earlier than the behavioural models assumed. The fix was to clock the comparator on clkb33 and drive the DAC switches from its SR latch.

cost ≈ 4 dB fast-path SNDR · 2026-07-25

2

Output polarity hidden by self-consistent checks

The DFF's Q and Q̄ were swapped. Golden netlist, schematic and LVS were all generated from the same subcircuit, so they agreed with each other. The first closed-loop extracted simulation railed. Block testbenches now check output sense, not only timing.

loop railed · 2026-07-20

3

Clock edge rate inside the chip

The comparator saw a 545 ps clk33 rise after level shifter and wiring; the block had been verified with 100 ps edges. Doubling the level shifter's output PMOS brought it to 317 ps.

cost 1.3 dB · 2026-07-25

4

Estimator variance sets the gate

512-bit windows scattered ±3 dB between runs of the same netlist, larger than the effects being chased. He moved to 2048-bit windows and sets each gate 2 dB below the measured value.

a lucky 36.2 dB draw passed a netlist whose real value was 32.3 dB

5

Transient runs contain no device noise

A separate .noise bench found the OTA 1/f corner at 631 kHz, covering the whole precision band. Input-referred noise is 126 µV against a 351 µV quantization floor, so about 0.5 dB.

2026-07-26

6

Magic reports DRC wrongly in both directions

A freshly loaded cell needs select top cell; expand before DRC, or hierarchy checks are skipped and it reads clean. writeall force after load halves coordinates in unmodified subcells and creates hundreds of fake violations.

894 fake violations chased · 2026-07-19

7

Extraction output order changes run to run

ext2spice writes parasitic caps in hash order, so each extraction gave ngspice a differently ordered matrix and an occasional convergence failure. He now sorts the netlist into a canonical form, commits it, and diffs it in CI.

nightly aborts · 2026-08-01

One gap: the integrator monitor pin load

sim/top_tb.py line 117 loads ua[1] with 50 fF. On silicon the integrator output drives the TT mux path directly: under 500 Ω and under 5 pF, plus board and probe. The OTA was sized for about 2.2 pF total (1.5 pF plus 0.72 pF extracted parasitic), and extracted PM is already 46°.

A few pF more, partly isolated by the 500 Ω switch, could lower GBW and phase margin. The size of the effect is not simulated. If we expose an integrator monitor, buffer it (TilesOS does) or simulate the real pin load.

What this means for our design

Carries over

  • Ratiometric references: ladder, followers and decap, with decap sized so each pulse sees a recovered reference.
  • Pin plan: signal on an analog pin, clock and bitstream on digital pins, references inferred rather than bought.
  • Frame hardening: tie unused outputs, run antenna checks, prove frame connectivity, set both uses_vapwr and uses_3v3.
  • Verification habits: closed-loop polarity check, comparator sampling instant, estimator variance, separate noise bench, canonical extracted netlist.

Does not carry over

  • His choice of CT is what makes the analog pin path harmless. Our switched-capacitor input samples through it, so the 500 Ω / 5 pF path belongs in the sampling settling budget.
  • He has no on-chip decimator; src/project.v is empty. Our decimator uses the digital area he leaves unused.
  • Single-ended with no CMFB. Our fully-differential OTA needs CMFB, which none of the TT references has done.
  • First-order pattern tones limit his precision path; our higher-order incremental loop avoids that class.
