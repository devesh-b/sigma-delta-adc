## Verdict

- **The gm/Id table and `gm_ratio` are sound.** The ×1.5 ratio of 1.283 at gm/Id 14 agrees with a hand EKV calculation to within 1 %.
- **The s5f conclusion that "the bases must rise" is wrong in practice, though not in arithmetic.**
  - If you require base GBW1 ≥ 14 MHz and base GBW2 ≥ 17.5 MHz at every corner with minimum current, the input pairs must reach gm/Id 14 and 16 at 125 °C.
  - That needs W/L ≈ 150 (OTA1) and ≈ 700 (OTA2) at L = 1 µm. The resulting Cgs breaks the β budgets.
  - The self-consistent method is to gate the **boosted operating point directly**, at every corner. Targets are in §Q2.
- **Fast mode is feasible inside 200 µA** (estimate ≈ 160–178 µA). The cheapest robust option is **OTA2 tail ×2** (about +13 µA in fast mode, no area cost).
- **The non-monotonic INL is a pattern, not a one-off.** In every comparable pair of rows, a faster OTA2 at fixed slew rate gave worse INL. The D39 "≥" rows are proven sufficient at only one point, but the cold corner runs about 1.5× above that GBW. This has to be resolved before S7.
- **S6 (golden vectors) is not blocked. The S7 SPEC freeze is blocked** by M1–M3 and the SPEC text updates.

## 1. Correctness

### Major

**M1. The s5f base-GBW sizing is over-constrained. Gate the boosted operating point instead.**
- **Location:** `work/v1-design/results/s5f_tier1.md`, "Bases needed"; SPEC.md l.93/95/116–120.
- **Problem:**
  - In the deck, gm/Id and GBW/SR are tied together. The tanh linear range is ip/gm, which gives gm/Id(device) = 4π·GBW/SR.
  - Check against the deck: OTA1 10 MHz / 12.5 V/µs → 10.05; OTA2 15 MHz / 13.5 V/µs → 13.96. These are exactly the "base gm/Id 10 / 14" used in fast3.
  - So "OTA1 base ≥ 14 MHz" at minimum current (17.5 µA tail = 12.5 V/µs × 1.4 pF) needs gm/Id = 4π·14/12.5 = 14.1 at the hot corner.
    - At ff/125 that means J ≈ 5.8e-8 A/sq. The table gives 15.7 at 1e-8 and 13.6 at 1e-7.
    - W/L = 8.75 µA / 5.8e-8 ≈ 150, so W·L ≈ 150 µm² at L = 1 µm.
    - Cgs ≈ 0.5–0.6 pF, against a total Cp_vg1 budget of 0.262 pF (β1 0.84).
  - "OTA2 base ≥ 17.5 MHz" needs gm/Id 16.3 at 125 °C. That is J ≈ 7e-9 at ff/125, so W/L ≈ 700.
  - The bound itself is valid (min(a·b) ≥ min(a)·min(b)). It just leads to devices that cannot be built.
- **Why the rows are fine anyway:**
  - The `NV_same` (bstgm = bst) row that passes at 3.7 ppm *is* the boosted operating point at the hot corner:
    - OTA1: 35 µA, 20 MHz, 25 V/µs, so gm/Id 10.05 at the boosted current.
    - OTA2: 14.2 µA, 22.5 MHz, 20.25 V/µs, so gm/Id 13.96.
  - Only that point matters. The base mode only has to meet its own ≥ 10 MHz row, and at hot the boost ratio is larger (closer to weak inversion).
- **PROVEN** (hand interpolation of the table; the method reproduces the tool's 1.328 for OTA2_17_x1.5 at ff/−40, L = 2).
- **Direction:** restate the C1 targets as "gm/Id at the boosted current density ≥ X at every corner", with X = 2π·GBW_abs·C_L/(I_tail,boost/2). The sizing targets are in §Q2.

**M2. The "≥" boosted-GBW rows are proven at one point only, and INL rises with OTA2 speed at fixed SR.**
- **Location:** `s5f_tier1.md`, V5(i) table; SPEC l.93/95.
- **Problem:**
  - Sorted by boosted GBW2 (base × ratio), the data rise:

  - Only the ×2 row breaks the trend (22.7 MHz, 7.75 / 7.57), and it also has the higher SR.
  - With the M1 sizing, the cold corner runs at OTA1 ≈ 30 MHz and OTA2 ≈ 34 MHz boosted, at unchanged SR (constant current, gm/Id ≈ 15–16 at ff/−40). No deck point covers that.
- **Why it matters:** if INL rises with GBW, a "≥ 22.5 MHz" gate can pass a design that fails at −40 °C.
- **PROVEN** (trend from the s5f table). The cold-corner GBWs are **REASONED** (table interpolation, constant tail current).

**M3. There is no β2 / Cp_vg2 row, but the evidence assumes β2 = 0.497.**
- **Location:** SPEC.md l.95 (OTA2 row has C_L ≈ 0.7 pF and no β). In `gen_tier1.py:98`, `cp = P.TIER1_CP_VG` (0.262 pF) is applied to vg2 as well.
- **Problem:** with option A at minimum current (×1.5), OTA2 needs W/L ≥ 106 (§Q2). At L = 1 µm:
  - W·L·Cox ≈ 0.85 pF (Cox ≈ 8 fF/µm², to be checked against the model's toxe), giving Cgs ≈ 0.35–0.45 pF.
  - β2 = 1/(1 + 0.75 + Cgs + switches) ≈ 0.45, against the deck's 0.497.
  - That is about 9 % less closed-loop bandwidth, so the 22.5 MHz row no longer guarantees the settling the deck showed.
- **REASONED** (Cox and the Cgs fraction are approximate).
- **Direction:** add an OTA2 row "Cp_vg2 ≤ 0.262 pF per side (β2 ≥ 0.497) incl. Cgs, switches, routing", or gate on β2·GBW2. C1 confirms with an `.op` Cgg.

## Answers to the questions

### Q1. gm/Id method

- **Same W/L, Id×k, strong-inversion side of the peak:** correct for a tail-current boost (`tier1_tb.py:1440–1446`).
- **Worst-corner selection:** the minimum over corners of the ratio, each corner at matched gm/Id. That is a conservative bound.
  - The physical case is fixed W/L and fixed J. Example: J from tt/27 at gm/Id 14 is J0 ≈ 3.7e-7. It gives ratios 1.34 (ff/−40) and 1.36 (ff/125), both above 1.283.
  - But the absolute boosted gm/Id at ff/125 is then about 10.5, not 12. So ratio-based gating hides the real binding constraint: the absolute gm/Id at the hot corner (M1).
- **Credibility:** PROVEN by hand calculation.
  - Peak 30.3 at 27 °C means n_eff = 38.7/30.3 = 1.28, which is plausible.
  - EKV check at ff/−40 (1/(nUT) = 39.5): gm/Id 14 gives IC = 5.14; ×1.5 gives IC 7.7 and gm/Id 11.9; ratio 1.275 against the table's 1.283.
  - OTA1 at gm/Id 10, ×2: EKV gives 1.48 against the table's 1.42. The lower table value is consistent with velocity saturation at L = 1 µm.
- **need_22p5:** the wrong quantity (m1). It should report the smallest base gm/Id, and preferably be replaced by the boosted-point target.

### Q3. Fast mode inside 200 µA

Feasible (REASONED).

| Option | Fast-mode current | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Option A as D39 (2.7·I_tail) | 2.7·(35 + 14.2) + ≈ 26 overhead ≈ 159 µA | If fold/CMFB are sized for boost and only the tail switches: ≈ 159 µA boost, ≈ 136 µA base |
| A′, OTA2 ×2 | 2.7·(35 + 18.9) + 26 ≈ 172 µA (≈ 178 with fixed fold) | Adds ≈ +13 µA |

Cheapest options, in order:
1. **OTA2 ×2 (A′).**
   - Cost: ≈ +13 µA in fast mode only, about zero area.
   - Gains: β2 safe, SR margin 33 %, small pair, gm/Id 10.5, well clear of the plateau.
2. **OTA2 ×1.5 with L ≈ 0.5 µm, W/L ≈ 110.**
   - Cost: 0 µA, about 30 µm² of gate.
   - Risks: there is no gm/Id data at L = 0.5; at 27 °C the operating point sits on the plateau region (m2); A0 has to be checked.
3. **Constant-gm bias for the OTA legs** (gm ∝ 1/R, so GBW is flat over temperature).
   - This removes the hot-corner sizing penalty, and the poly-R tolerance (≈ ±15–20 %) becomes the corner instead.
   - Current rises at hot. A good learning step, but it is a C6 change.
4. **Relax N1024 fast-mode INL.** Zero cost.
   - The minimum-base rows give 7.6–12.5 ppm, so ≤ 15 ppm would accept the present option A without resizing.
   - This only relaxes the fast mode (N1024 ≤ 5 → ≤ 15 ppm); precision mode is untouched.

Every option still needs M2 resolved (the cold-corner run).

### Q4. Non-monotonic INL vs GBW2

- **No bug in `gen_tier1.py:240–260`.**
  - gm follows bstgm; ip follows bst·SR; g0 = gm/A0 keeps A0 constant; fp2 tracks gm, so PM is constant.
  - This is consistent with `test_bstgm_default_is_bit_identical…`.
- **A lone OTA2 should improve monotonically.**
  - For one slew-then-settle stage with fp2 ∝ gm, the dynamics normalised to τ = C_L/(β·gm) do not change with scale. The tanh linear range in units of β·SR·τ is constant, and so is the fp2 lag relative to τ.
  - The only absolute quantity is slew time (step/SR), and more gm shortens the linear phase needed after it.
  - Hand check: 1.13 V step, 20.25 V/µs, 100 ns phase. Settling error is ≈ 8.8 mV at 19.2 MHz and ≈ 2.8 mV at 25.7 MHz.
- **Hypotheses, ranked (REASONED):**
  - **(a) Signed cancellation between stages under the max-norm endpoint metric.** OTA1 is under-spec (14.2 MHz) in every V5i row. A faster OTA2 removes a stage-2 term that was partly cancelling OTA1's term.
  - **(b) Numerical.** Higher gm/ip makes the tanh stiffer, and errors of order reltol accumulate over N cycles.
  - **(c) Pattern-dependent residue error.** Coefficient error δ times the quantisation residue (about 244 ppm raw floor at N256, per F10) is not smooth in u. With 21 points this shows up as erratic ±ppm. It fits N256 better than N1024.
- **Discriminating runs** (each about 2 deck jobs):
  1. OTA1 ideal (GBW1 1 GHz, SR1 1e3 V/µs) with GBW2 at 15 / 20 / 25 MHz. Monotone → (a).
  2. reltol 1e-5 and maxstep halved on the 15 vs 20 rows. The gap moves → (b).
  3. fp2_mult 30. If the gap persists, the second pole is not the cause.
  4. Signed INL(u) over 101 points for both rows, plus a third-order-fit residual. Smooth → (a); jagged → (c).

## 2. Efficiency

- **E1. OTA2 ×2 instead of a ×1.5 large pair.** Costs +13 µA in fast mode only. Avoids about 0.3 pF on vg2 (keeps β2 ≥ 0.497) and roughly 75 µm² of input-pair gate area at L = 1 µm. Adds 33 % SR margin.
- **E2. Keep the OTA1 pair at L ≈ 1 µm, W ≈ 30 µm.** Larger L costs β1 directly (Cgs ∝ L² at fixed J). The 1/f penalty is absorbed by chopping.
- **E3. Relax fast-mode N1024 to ≤ 15 ppm.** Zero µA and zero area. Fast-mode linearity goes from 5 to about 13 ppm.
